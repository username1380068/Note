Eigenvalues of Polarized Endomorphisms on the Néron–Severi Space

1. The norm defined by the cone

Let MM be a nonzero finite-dimensional real vector space, and let C⊂MC\subset M be a closed convex cone containing no line. Fix h∈int⁡MCh\in\operatorname{int}_M C and define ‖x‖h=inf⁡{t≥0:th±x∈C},x∈M.\lVert x\rVert_h=\inf\{t\ge0:th\pm x\in C\}, \qquad x\in M.

  1. Positive definiteness. If ‖x‖h=0\lVert x\rVert_h=0, then closedness gives x,−x∈Cx,-x\in C, so x=0x=0.

  2. Homogeneity. Rescaling tt and interchanging the signs gives ‖λx‖h=|λ|‖x‖h\lVert \lambda x\rVert_h=|\lambda|\lVert x\rVert_h.

  3. Triangle inequality. If th±x∈Cth\pm x\in C and sh±y∈Csh\pm y\in C, then (t+s)h±(x+y)∈C(t+s)h\pm(x+y)\in C. Taking infima over tt and ss gives ‖x+y‖h≤‖x‖h+‖y‖h\lVert x+y\rVert_h\le\lVert x\rVert_h+\lVert y\rVert_h.

2. The induced operator norm

Every norm ∥⋅∥\|\cdot\| on MM induces an operator norm on End⁡(M)\operatorname{End}(M), given by ‖A‖op=supx≠0∥Ax∥∥x∥=sup∥x∥=1∥Ax∥.\lVert A\rVert_{\mathrm{op}} =\sup_{x\ne0}\frac{\|Ax\|}{\|x\|} =\sup_{\|x\|=1}\|Ax\|. The supremum is finite because MM is finite-dimensional. Below, ∥⋅∥op\|\cdot\|_{\mathrm{op}} is induced by ∥⋅∥h\|\cdot\|_h.

3. Isometry and recurrence

Proposition. Suppose T:M→MT\colon M\to M is an invertible linear map, T(C)=CT(C)=C, and Th=hTh=h. Then ‖Tx‖h=‖x‖h\lVert Tx\rVert_h=\lVert x\rVert_h for every x∈Mx\in M, and there are positive integers ri→∞r_i\to\infty such that ‖Tri−IM‖op→0.\lVert T^{r_i}-I_M\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\longrightarrow 0.

Proof. Since T(C)=CT(C)=C and Th=hTh=h, applying TT gives, for every t≥0t\ge0, th±x∈C⇔th±Tx∈C.th\pm x\in C \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad th\pm Tx\in C. Taking infima gives ‖Tx‖h=‖x‖h\lVert Tx\rVert_h=\lVert x\rVert_h. Thus ‖Tmx‖h=‖x‖h\lVert T^m x\rVert_h=\lVert x\rVert_h, so ‖Tm‖op=1\lVert T^m\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}=1 for every m≥1m\ge1.

The bounded sequence (Tm)m≥1(T^m)_{m\ge1} in the finite-dimensional space End⁡(M)\operatorname{End}(M) has a convergent subsequence (Tmi)i≥1(T^{m_i})_{i\ge1} with mim_i strictly increasing. Set ri=m2i−mi≥ir_i=m_{2i}-m_i\ge i. As TmiT^{m_i} maps the unit sphere bijectively onto itself, ‖ATmi‖op=‖A‖op\lVert AT^{m_i}\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}=\lVert A\rVert_{\mathrm{op}} for every A∈End⁡(M)A\in\operatorname{End}(M). It follows that ‖Tri−IM‖op=‖(Tri−IM)Tmi‖op=‖Tm2i−Tmi‖op→0,\begin{aligned} \lVert T^{r_i}-I_M\rVert_{\mathrm{op}} &=\lVert (T^{r_i}-I_M)T^{m_i}\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\\ &=\lVert T^{m_{2i}}-T^{m_i}\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\longrightarrow 0, \end{aligned} since Tm2iT^{m_{2i}} and TmiT^{m_i} converge to the same limit. ◻

Proof. Set S=q−1TS=q^{-1}T. Then S(C)=CS(C)=C and Sh=hSh=h, so Sri→IMS^{r_i}\to I_M by the proposition. This convergence holds entrywise in any basis and hence also for the complexifications.
