Divisorial Proj on surfaces

On a normal algebraic surface, the relative Proj of a divisorial algebra is exactly the locus where the divisor is Q\mathbb Q-Cartier. Finite generation is equivalent to the divisor being Q\mathbb Q-Cartier everywhere; without it, the Proj can omit finitely many points.

Let XX be a normal integral algebraic surface over a field, and let BB be an integral Weil divisor. Set

R(B)=⨁m≥0OX(mB),f:Y=Proj⁡XR(B)⟶X.\begin{gathered} \mathcal R(B)=\bigoplus_{m\ge0}\mathcal O_X(mB),\\ f:Y=\operatorname{Proj}_X\mathcal R(B)\longrightarrow X. \end{gathered}

The algebra uses the full divisorial sheaves OX(mB)\mathcal O_X(mB), or equivalently the reflexive powers of OX(B)\mathcal O_X(B). Finite generation below always means local finite generation as an OX\mathcal O_X-algebra, not finite generation of the global section ring.

The relative Proj

Let UB⊂XU_B\subset X be the Q\mathbb Q-Cartier locus of BB: a point belongs to UBU_B if some positive multiple of BB is Cartier on a neighbourhood of that point. This is an open subset containing every codimension-one point of XX.

Proposition 1. The morphism ff identifies YY with UBU_B. In particular, for every point x∈Xx\in X,

f−1(x)={Spec⁡κ(x),x∈UB,∅,x∉UB.f^{-1}(x)= \begin{cases} \operatorname{Spec}\kappa(x),&x\in U_B,\\ \varnothing,&x\notin U_B. \end{cases}

No finite-generation assumption is required.

Proof

Step 1. Standard affine charts

Each standard Proj chart is the spectrum of the regular functions on the complement of an effective Weil divisor.

Proof of Step 1

Work over an affine open subset V=Spec⁡A⊂XV=\operatorname{Spec}A\subset X, put K=Frac⁡AK=\operatorname{Frac}A, and write Rm=Γ(V,OV(mB))⊂KR_m=\Gamma(V,\mathcal O_V(mB))\subset K. For a nonzero homogeneous element s∈Rds\in R_d of positive degree, set

Hs=div⁡V(s)+dB∣V≥0,Us=V∖Supp⁡Hs.\begin{gathered} H_s=\operatorname{div}_V(s)+dB|_V\ge0,\\ U_s=V\setminus\operatorname{Supp}H_s. \end{gathered}

Here ss is regarded as a rational function when forming its divisor. The standard affine chart has coordinate ring

R(s)=⋃n≥0s−nRnd=Γ(Us,OUs)⊂K.\begin{aligned} R_{(s)}&=\bigcup_{n\ge0}s^{-n}R_{nd}\\ &=\Gamma(U_s,\mathcal O_{U_s})\subset K. \end{aligned}

Indeed, for g∈K×g\in K^\times,

g∈s−nRnd⟺div⁡V(g)+nHs≥0.\begin{gathered} g\in s^{-n}R_{nd}\\ \Longleftrightarrow\quad\operatorname{div}_V(g)+nH_s\ge0. \end{gathered}

Such a function is regular on UsU_s. Conversely, a regular function on UsU_s can have poles on VV only along the finitely many prime components of HsH_s. Their coefficients in HsH_s are positive, so a sufficiently large nn gives the displayed inequality. Normality is used to test regularity at codimension-one points. Consequently,

D+(s)=Spec⁡Γ(Us,OUs).D_+(s)=\operatorname{Spec}\Gamma(U_s,\mathcal O_{U_s}).

Step 2. Affineness and gluing

Nagata’s affineness theorem for normal surfaces turns these charts into open subsets of the base.

Proof of Step 2

We use the following form of Nagata’s theorem: if VV is an excellent normal integral affine surface and HH is an effective Weil divisor, then V∖Supp⁡HV\setminus\operatorname{Supp}H is affine. The case H=0H=0 is immediate. See [B], Introduction, p. 1. Algebraic surfaces over a field are excellent, so the theorem applies here.

Thus UsU_s is affine, and the calculation in Step 1 identifies the structural morphism D+(s)→VD_+(s)\to V with the open immersion Us↪VU_s\hookrightarrow V. These identifications agree on overlaps: for nonzero homogeneous s,ts,t,

D+(s)∩D+(t)=D+(st),Hst=Hs+Ht,\begin{gathered} D_+(s)\cap D_+(t)=D_+(st),\\ H_{st}=H_s+H_t, \end{gathered}

so both overlaps correspond to Us∩UtU_s\cap U_t. All restriction maps are the natural maps inside KK. The chart identifications therefore glue, and ff is an open immersion.

Step 3. Identifying the image

The image consists precisely of the points where a positive multiple of the divisor is locally principal.

Proof of Step 3

On UsU_s, the effective divisor HsH_s vanishes. Hence

dB∣Us=−div⁡Us(s),dB|_{U_s}=-\operatorname{div}_{U_s}(s),

so every chart image lies in UBU_B.

Conversely, suppose x∈UBx\in U_B. On a neighbourhood WW of xx, choose r>0r>0 such that rB∣WrB|_W is Cartier. The Veronese algebra is then

R(B)∣W(r)=⨁n≥0OW(nrB)≃Sym⁡OWOW(rB).\begin{aligned} \mathcal R(B)|_W^{(r)} &=\bigoplus_{n\ge0}\mathcal O_W(nrB)\\ &\simeq\operatorname{Sym}_{\mathcal O_W}\mathcal O_W(rB). \end{aligned}

Passing to a Veronese algebra does not change Proj, and the relative Proj of the symmetric algebra of an invertible sheaf is the base itself. Thus f−1(W)≃Wf^{-1}(W)\simeq W, and xx lies in the image. This proves Y≃UBY\simeq U_B, including the assertion about fibres.

□

Finite generation and properness

Corollary 2. The following conditions are equivalent:

  1. R(B)\mathcal R(B) is locally finitely generated as an OX\mathcal O_X-algebra.
  2. BB is Q\mathbb Q-Cartier.
  3. ff is proper.
  4. ff is surjective.
  5. ff is an isomorphism.

Proof

By Proposition 1, ff is the inclusion UB↪XU_B\hookrightarrow X, so (2), (4), and (5) are equivalent. A proper open immersion has closed image. Since UBU_B is dense and XX is integral, (3) implies (4); conversely, (5) implies (3).

If (1) holds, the relative Proj is proper over XX: this can be checked locally on the base, where a sufficiently divisible Veronese algebra is generated in degree one and gives a closed immersion into a projective space. Thus (1) implies (3).

Finally assume (2). Locally choose r>0r>0 with rBrB Cartier. The algebra R(B)(r)≃Sym⁡OX(rB)\mathcal R(B)^{(r)}\simeq\operatorname{Sym}\mathcal O_X(rB) is of finite type. For 0≤i<r0\le i<r,

OX((nr+i)B)≃OX(iB)⊗OX(nrB).\mathcal O_X((nr+i)B) \simeq\mathcal O_X(iB)\otimes\mathcal O_X(nrB).

Each OX(iB)\mathcal O_X(iB) is coherent, so the finitely many residue-degree pieces generate R(B)\mathcal R(B) as a finite module over R(B)(r)\mathcal R(B)^{(r)}. This gives (1).

□

In particular, XX is Q\mathbb Q-factorial if and only if R(B)\mathcal R(B) is locally finitely generated for every integral Weil divisor BB.

Remark. A proper birational morphism between normal algebraic surfaces with no exceptional curves is an isomorphism: its fibres are zero-dimensional, hence it is finite, and a finite birational morphism to a normal surface is an isomorphism. Properness is essential in this argument. The inclusion UB↪XU_B\hookrightarrow X need not be surjective.

Finite type of the Proj is also weaker than finite generation of the algebra. Proposition 1 shows that YY is always a Noetherian surface and ff is of finite type, since it is an open immersion into the Noetherian scheme XX. When BB is not Q\mathbb Q-Cartier, the algebra is nevertheless not finitely generated. Its Proj omits the non-Q\mathbb Q-Cartier points, a finite subset of the singular locus.

An elliptic cone

Work over C\mathbb C. Let C⊂P2C\subset\mathbb P^2 be a smooth plane cubic, put L=OC(1)L=\mathcal O_C(1), and let

X=Spec⁡⨁n≥0H0(C,L⊗n)X=\operatorname{Spec}\bigoplus_{n\ge0}H^0(C,L^{\otimes n})

be its normal affine cone, with vertex vv. Choose p,q∈Cp,q\in C such that M=OC(p−q)M=\mathcal O_C(p-q) is non-torsion in Pic⁡0(C)\operatorname{Pic}^0(C). Let ℓp,ℓq⊂X\ell_p,\ell_q\subset X be the corresponding ruling lines, and set B=ℓp−ℓqB=\ell_p-\ell_q.

Example 3. The divisor BB is Cartier away from vv, but is not Q\mathbb Q-Cartier at vv. Consequently,

Proj⁡XR(B)≃X∖{v},\operatorname{Proj}_X\mathcal R(B)\simeq X\setminus\{v\},

and R(B)\mathcal R(B) is not locally finitely generated at vv.

Proof

The punctured cone is smooth, so BB is Cartier there. Blowing up the vertex gives

π:X~=Tot⁡(L−1)⟶X,\pi:\widetilde X=\operatorname{Tot}(L^{-1})\longrightarrow X,

with exceptional zero section E≃CE\simeq C. For the strict transform B~\widetilde B,

OX~(B~)∣E≃M,OX~(E)∣E≃L−1.\mathcal O_{\widetilde X}(\widetilde B)|_E\simeq M, \qquad \mathcal O_{\widetilde X}(E)|_E\simeq L^{-1}.

If mBmB were Cartier near vv for some m>0m>0, it would be principal on a smaller neighbourhood. Comparing with its strict transform gives, on the inverse image of that neighbourhood,

π∗(mB)=mB~+aE\pi^*(mB)=m\widetilde B+aE

for some integer aa. Restriction to EE would yield

M⊗m⊗L−a≃OC.M^{\otimes m}\otimes L^{-a}\simeq\mathcal O_C.

Taking degrees gives a=0a=0, because deg⁡M=0\deg M=0 and deg⁡L=3\deg L=3. Then M⊗mM^{\otimes m} would be trivial, contradicting the choice of MM. The assertions about Proj and finite generation follow from Proposition 1 and Corollary 2.

□
