pullback

The de Fernex–Hacon asymptotic pullback extends Cartier pullback to arbitrary Weil divisors. Its basic structure is monotone, positively homogeneous, and subadditive; additivity, negation, and composition require care.

Throughout, the ground field is algebraically closed of characteristic zero, and π:Y→X\pi:Y\to X is a projective birational morphism of normal varieties. Unless specified otherwise, D,D′D,D' are Weil R\mathbb R-divisors. All inequalities, limits, infima, and suprema are coefficientwise. The strict transform is denoted by D~\widetilde D.

The 19 property numbers agree with the accompanying note. Source numbers and pages below refer to the supplied manuscript Finite Generation under Int-Amplified Endomorphisms, version finite-generation-and-equivariant-lc-models(4).pdf. Its geometric hypotheses apply throughout; quasi-projectivity or projectivity is added where needed. Proofs first establish rational statements, then use Property 10 for real coefficients.

Definition and natural pullback

For integral DD, define the natural pullback by

OY(−π♮D)=(OX(−D)⋅OY)∨∨.\mathcal O_Y(-\pi^\natural D)=\bigl(\mathcal O_X(-D)\cdot\mathcal O_Y\bigr)^{\vee\vee}.

If sis_i generate OX(−D)\mathcal O_X(-D) near the center of EE, then coeff⁡E(π♮D)=min⁡iord⁡E(si)\operatorname{coeff}_E(\pi^\natural D)=\min_i\operatorname{ord}_E(s_i). Comparing fractional ideals at these discrete valuation rings shows that natural pullback is monotone and subadditive. Multiplication by a local equation of a Cartier divisor CC gives π♮(D+C)=π♮D+π∗C\pi^\natural(D+C)=\pi^\natural D+\pi^*C. These elementary facts are the starting point of the proofs below.

Property 1. Existence of the asymptotic limit

For an integral Weil divisor DD, the coefficientwise limit exists and is a Weil R\mathbb R-divisor:

π∗D:=πdFH∗D=lim⁡m→∞π♮(mD)m=inf⁡m≥1π♮(mD)m.\pi^*D:=\pi^*_{\mathrm{dFH}}D=\lim_{m\to\infty}\frac{\pi^\natural(mD)}m=\inf_{m\ge1}\frac{\pi^\natural(mD)}m.

Lemma 1.6, p. 2

Proof of Property 1

Fix a prime divisor E⊂YE\subset Y and put bm=coeff⁡E(π♮(mD))b_m=\operatorname{coeff}_E(\pi^\natural(mD)). The natural-pullback inequality for sums gives bm+n≤bm+bnb_{m+n}\le b_m+b_n.

Choose an affine open set UU containing the generic point of the center of EE, and a nonzero section s∈H0(U,OU(D))s\in H^0(U,\mathcal O_U(D)). Then C=−div⁡U(s)C=-\operatorname{div}_U(s) is Cartier and C≤D∣UC\le D|_U. Monotonicity of natural pullback gives bm/m≥coeff⁡EU(πU∗C)b_m/m\ge\operatorname{coeff}_{E_U}(\pi_U^*C), a finite lower bound independent of mm.

Here is the needed form of Fekete's argument. Set b0=0b_0=0. For a fixed k≥1k\ge1, write m=qk+rm=qk+r with 0≤r<k0\le r<k. Subadditivity gives

bmm≤bkk+br−rbk/km.\frac{b_m}{m}\le\frac{b_k}{k}+\frac{b_r-rb_k/k}{m}.

The last numerator is bounded as rr varies. Therefore lim sup⁡mbm/m≤bk/k\limsup_m b_m/m\le b_k/k for every kk. Taking the infimum over kk, and using inf⁡kbk/k≤lim inf⁡mbm/m\inf_k b_k/k\le\liminf_m b_m/m, proves existence and the infimum formula.

Away from the strict transform of Supp⁡D\operatorname{Supp}D and the exceptional prime divisors of π\pi, all these coefficients vanish. This is a fixed finite union, so the coefficientwise limits form a Weil R\mathbb R-divisor, rather than an infinite formal sum.

□

Property 2. Comparison with natural pullbacks

For integral DD and each integer m≥1m\ge1,

π∗D≤π♮(mD)m≤π♮D.\pi^*D\le\frac{\pi^\natural(mD)}m\le\pi^\natural D.

If mDmD is Cartier, then π∗D=m−1π♮(mD)\pi^*D=m^{-1}\pi^\natural(mD).

Lemma 1.7(1)–(2), p. 2

Proof of Property 2

The first inequality follows from the infimum formula in Property 1. Repeated use of natural subadditivity gives π♮(mD)≤mπ♮D\pi^\natural(mD)\le m\pi^\natural D, proving the second.

If mDmD is Cartier, then for every k≥1k\ge1 its natural pullback is its usual pullback and

π♮(kmD)=kπ♮(mD).\pi^\natural(kmD)=k\pi^\natural(mD).

Taking the limit defining π∗D\pi^*D along the subsequence of multiples of mm gives the claimed equality. The comparison inequalities do not assert that π♮(mD)/m\pi^\natural(mD)/m is monotone in all positive integers mm; Example A below shows that it need not be.

Basic operations

Property 3. Monotonicity and effectivity

If D≤D′D\le D', then π∗D≤π∗D′\pi^*D\le\pi^*D'. In particular, D≥0D\ge0 implies π∗D≥0\pi^*D\ge0.

Lemmas 1.9(1) and 1.12(3), pp. 3–4

Proof of Property 3

For integral divisors, D≤D′D\le D' gives OX(−mD′)⊆OX(−mD)\mathcal O_X(-mD')\subseteq\mathcal O_X(-mD). The minimum valuation of a smaller fractional ideal is no smaller. Hence π♮(mD)≤π♮(mD′)\pi^\natural(mD)\le\pi^\natural(mD'). Divide by mm and pass to the limit. Clear a common denominator for rational divisors.

For real divisors, take the ordered rational approximations Dj=⌈jD⌉/jD_j=\lceil jD\rceil/j and Dj′=⌈jD′⌉/jD'_j=\lceil jD'\rceil/j. They have fixed finite support and satisfy Dj≤Dj′D_j\le D'_j. The real extension in Property 10 gives π∗D≤π∗D′\pi^*D\le\pi^*D'. Finally, π∗0=0\pi^*0=0, so comparison with zero proves effectivity.

□

Property 4. Positive homogeneity

For every real t≥0t\ge0,

π∗(tD)=tπ∗D.\pi^*(tD)=t\pi^*D.

Lemma 1.9(4), with Lemma 1.15, pp. 3, 5

Proof of Property 4

If DD is integral and kk is a positive integer, Property 1 yields

π∗(kD)=lim⁡m→∞π♮(mkD)m=klim⁡m→∞π♮(mkD)mk=kπ∗D.\pi^*(kD)=\lim_{m\to\infty}\frac{\pi^\natural(mkD)}m=k\lim_{m\to\infty}\frac{\pi^\natural(mkD)}{mk}=k\pi^*D.

The subsequence has the same limit because the full sequence converges. Thus π∗D=q−1π∗(qD)\pi^*D=q^{-1}\pi^*(qD) defines the rational pullback independently of the denominator. Clearing denominators now proves the formula for rational t>0t>0 and rational DD.

For real t>0t>0 and real DD, choose positive rational tj→tt_j\to t and rational Dj→DD_j\to D on a fixed finite support. Then tjDj→tDt_jD_j\to tD on that support. Property 10 gives

π∗(tD)=lim⁡jπ∗(tjDj)=lim⁡jtjπ∗Dj=tπ∗D.\pi^*(tD)=\lim_j\pi^*(t_jD_j)=\lim_j t_j\pi^*D_j=t\pi^*D.

The case t=0t=0 is immediate. Negative scalars are excluded: Property 7 is generally an inequality.

Property 5. Subadditivity

π∗(D+D′)≤π∗D+π∗D′.\pi^*(D+D')\le\pi^*D+\pi^*D'.

Lemma 1.9(2), with Lemma 1.15, pp. 3, 5

Proof of Property 5

For integral D,D′D,D' and every m≥1m\ge1, there is an inclusion

OX(−mD)OX(−mD′)⊆OX(−m(D+D′)).\mathcal O_X(-mD)\mathcal O_X(-mD')\subseteq\mathcal O_X(-m(D+D')).

After extension to YY, orders of the product add at the discrete valuation ring of any prime divisor. The inclusion therefore gives

π♮(m(D+D′))≤π♮(mD)+π♮(mD′).\pi^\natural(m(D+D'))\le\pi^\natural(mD)+\pi^\natural(mD').

Divide by mm and use Property 1. For rational divisors clear a common denominator. For real divisors choose rational approximations to both divisors supported in the union of their finite supports, and apply Property 10 to each term. Example B gives strict inequality.

Property 6. Cartier compatibility and translation

If CC is R\mathbb R-Cartier, then

π∗(D+C)=π∗D+π∗C,\pi^*(D+C)=\pi^*D+\pi^*C,

where π∗C\pi^*C on the right is the usual Cartier pullback. In particular, the two pullbacks agree on R\mathbb R-Cartier divisors. For h∈K(X)∗h\in K(X)^*,

π∗(D+div⁡Xh)=π∗D+div⁡Y(h∘π).\pi^*(D+\operatorname{div}_Xh)=\pi^*D+\operatorname{div}_Y(h\circ\pi).

Lemmas 1.9(3) and 1.12(2),(4), pp. 3–4

Proof of Property 6

For integral DD and Cartier CC, a local equation cc for CC gives

OX(−m(D+C))=cmOX(−mD).\mathcal O_X(-m(D+C))=c^m\mathcal O_X(-mD).

Thus π♮(m(D+C))=π♮(mD)+mπ∗C\pi^\natural(m(D+C))=\pi^\natural(mD)+m\pi^*C. Divide by mm and take limits. Clearing denominators proves the rational case.

Write a R\mathbb R-Cartier divisor as C=∑i=1rciCiC=\sum_{i=1}^r c_iC_i with CiC_i Cartier. Choose rational cij→cic_{ij}\to c_i, put Cj=∑icijCiC_j=\sum_i c_{ij}C_i, and choose rational approximations Dj→DD_j\to D. All supports lie in one finite set. Hence

π∗(D+C)=lim⁡jπ∗(Dj+Cj)=π∗D+∑iciπ∗Ci.\pi^*(D+C)=\lim_j\pi^*(D_j+C_j)=\pi^*D+\sum_i c_i\pi^*C_i.

Taking D=0D=0 identifies the asymptotic pullback of CC with the usual one. Taking C=div⁡XhC=\operatorname{div}_Xh proves the last formula and preservation of linear equivalence.

Property 7. Comparison under negation

−π∗(−D)≤π∗D.-\pi^*(-D)\le\pi^*D.

The difference π∗D+π∗(−D)\pi^*D+\pi^*(-D) is effective and π\pi-exceptional. It vanishes when DD is R\mathbb R-Cartier.

Lemma 1.9(5), with Lemma 1.15, pp. 3, 5

Proof of Property 7

Apply Property 5 to DD and −D-D:

0=π∗0≤π∗D+π∗(−D).0=\pi^*0\le\pi^*D+\pi^*(-D).

Along a nonexceptional prime divisor, each pullback agrees with the strict transform, so the coefficients of the sum cancel by Property 8. Hence the effective sum is exceptional. If DD is R\mathbb R-Cartier, both pullbacks are the usual ones by Property 6, and ordinary Cartier pullback commutes with negation. This is only a sufficient condition for equality; on surfaces equality holds for every Weil divisor by Property 17.

□

Property 8. Pushforward and strict transforms

π∗π∗D=D.\pi_*\pi^*D=D.

The coefficient of π∗D\pi^*D along every nonexceptional prime divisor equals its strict-transform coefficient. If π\pi is small, then π∗D=D~\pi^*D=\widetilde D.

Lemmas 1.7(3)–(4) and 1.15, pp. 2, 5

Proof of Property 8

A proper birational morphism to a normal variety is an isomorphism over an open subset containing all codimension-one points of the target. At the generic point of a prime divisor P⊂XP\subset X, the sheaf OX(−mD)\mathcal O_X(-mD) is the principal fractional ideal of order mcoeff⁡P(D)m\operatorname{coeff}_P(D) in the corresponding discrete valuation ring. Consequently, its natural pullback has that order along the strict transform of PP. Dividing by mm and taking the limit gives the assertion for integral divisors; clearing denominators and fixed-support approximation give it for real divisors.

Exceptional prime divisors have centers of codimension at least two and push forward to zero as divisors. Pushing forward therefore recovers DD. A small morphism has no exceptional prime divisors, so only the strict-transform part remains.

□

Property 9. Convexity

For 0≤t≤10\le t\le1,

π∗((1−t)D0+tD1)≤(1−t)π∗D0+tπ∗D1.\pi^*((1-t)D_0+tD_1)\le(1-t)\pi^*D_0+t\pi^*D_1.

For each fixed prime divisor E⊂YE\subset Y, the function D↦coeff⁡E(π∗D)D\mapsto\operatorname{coeff}_E(\pi^*D) is convex.

Direct consequence; used in Lemma 3.1, pp. 13–14

Proof of Property 9

Subadditivity and positive homogeneity give

π∗((1−t)D0+tD1)≤π∗((1−t)D0)+π∗(tD1)=(1−t)π∗D0+tπ∗D1.\pi^*((1-t)D_0+tD_1)\le\pi^*((1-t)D_0)+\pi^*(tD_1)=(1-t)\pi^*D_0+t\pi^*D_1.

Taking the coefficient of EE gives exactly the defining convexity inequality. The endpoint cases follow from π∗0=0\pi^*0=0. In particular, subtracting such a coefficient from a fixed canonical-divisor coefficient produces a concave function of the boundary, as used for discrepancies in the manuscript.

Continuity and Cartier bounds

Property 10. Continuity on a fixed finite support

Fix distinct prime divisors P1,…,Pr⊂XP_1,\ldots,P_r\subset X. For each E⊂YE\subset Y there is a constant ME≥0M_E\ge0 such that, for B=∑biPiB=\sum b_iP_i and B′=∑bi′PiB'=\sum b_i'P_i,

∣coeff⁡E(π∗B−π∗B′)∣≤MEmax⁡i∣bi−bi′∣.\left|\operatorname{coeff}_E(\pi^*B-\pi^*B')\right|\le M_E\max_i|b_i-b_i'|.

Thus Dj→DD_j\to D on a fixed finite support implies π∗Dj→π∗D\pi^*D_j\to\pi^*D coefficientwise. This gives a well-defined real-coefficient extension, compatible with restriction to open subsets.

Lemmas 1.10 and 1.15; Definition 1.11, pp. 3, 5

Proof of Property 10

First use only rational coefficients and the rational versions of Properties 3–5. Put P=∑iPiP=\sum_iP_i and δ=max⁡i∣bi−bi′∣\delta=\max_i|b_i-b_i'|. Since B≤B′+δPB\le B'+\delta P and B′≤B+δPB'\le B+\delta P,

−δπ∗P≤π∗B−π∗B′≤δπ∗P.-\delta\pi^*P\le\pi^*B-\pi^*B'\le\delta\pi^*P.

The divisor PP is effective, so ME=coeff⁡E(π∗P)≥0M_E=\operatorname{coeff}_E(\pi^*P)\ge0 gives the estimate.

Now let DjD_j be rational divisors on this support converging to a real divisor DD. The estimate makes every coefficient of π∗Dj\pi^*D_j Cauchy. All pullbacks have support in the strict transforms of the PiP_i and the finitely many exceptional prime divisors, so their limit is a Weil R\mathbb R-divisor. Applying the same estimate to two approximating sequences proves independence of the sequence. If two finite supports are used, take their union. Define π∗D\pi^*D to be this limit.

Passing to limits in the rational estimate proves the displayed bound for real coefficients and hence the asserted continuity. Extension of fractional ideals and their codimension-one orders commute with restriction to open sets. This proves locality for rational divisors, and passage to the defining limit proves it for real divisors. There is no circularity: only the rational forms of Properties 3–5 were used to construct the real extension.

□

Property 11. The local Cartier upper-bound formula

Let U⊂XU\subset X be affine with EU=E∩π−1(U)≠∅E_U=E\cap\pi^{-1}(U)\ne\varnothing, and write πU=π∣π−1(U)\pi_U=\pi|_{\pi^{-1}(U)}. Then

coeff⁡E(π∗D)=inf⁡C≥D∣UC Q-Cartiercoeff⁡EU(πU∗C).\operatorname{coeff}_E(\pi^*D)=\inf_{\substack{C\ge D|_U\\C\ \mathbb Q\text{-Cartier}}}\operatorname{coeff}_{E_U}(\pi_U^*C).

The same formula holds with R\mathbb R-Cartier upper bounds.

Lemma 1.12(1), p. 4

Proof of Property 11

Suppose first that DD is rational. For sufficiently divisible mm, the divisor mDmD is integral. Choose a finite set of affine generators of OU(−mD)\mathcal O_U(-mD) and a generator ss with minimum order along EUE_U. Then

Cm=1mdiv⁡U(s)≥D∣U,coeff⁡EU(πU∗Cm)=1mcoeff⁡EU(πU♮(mD)).C_m=\frac1m\operatorname{div}_U(s)\ge D|_U,\qquad \operatorname{coeff}_{E_U}(\pi_U^*C_m)=\frac1m\operatorname{coeff}_{E_U}(\pi_U^\natural(mD)).

The limit over divisible mm is coeff⁡E(π∗D)\operatorname{coeff}_E(\pi^*D), so the infimum is at most that value. Conversely, every Cartier upper bound C≥D∣UC\ge D|_U satisfies πU∗C≥πU∗(D∣U)\pi_U^*C\ge\pi_U^*(D|_U) by monotonicity and Cartier compatibility. This proves equality.

For real DD, set Dj=⌈jD⌉/jD_j=\lceil jD\rceil/j. The family of Q\mathbb Q-Cartier upper bounds for Dj∣UD_j|_U is contained in that for D∣UD|_U. Thus, writing I(D)I(D) for the latter infimum,

coeff⁡E(π∗D)≤I(D)≤I(Dj)=coeff⁡E(π∗Dj).\operatorname{coeff}_E(\pi^*D)\le I(D)\le I(D_j)=\operatorname{coeff}_E(\pi^*D_j).

Property 10 gives equality as j→∞j\to\infty. Finally, allowing R\mathbb R-Cartier divisors only enlarges the family, whereas monotonicity still bounds every value from below by coeff⁡E(π∗D)\operatorname{coeff}_E(\pi^*D). The real and rational infima therefore coincide.

Property 12. Global upper and lower envelopes

If X,YX,Y are normal and quasi-projective, then

−π∗(−D)=sup⁡C≤DC Q-Cartierπ∗C ≤ inf⁡C≥DC Q-Cartierπ∗C=π∗D.-\pi^*(-D)=\sup_{\substack{C\le D\\C\ \mathbb Q\text{-Cartier}}}\pi^*C\ \le\ \inf_{\substack{C\ge D\\C\ \mathbb Q\text{-Cartier}}}\pi^*C=\pi^*D.

Both identities also hold with R\mathbb R-Cartier bounds. All extrema are coefficientwise and need not be attained.

Lemma 1.13, pp. 4–5

Proof of Property 12

Fix EE and ε>0\varepsilon>0. By Property 11, on an affine open set UU meeting the center of EE choose a Q\mathbb Q-Cartier divisor B≥D∣UB\ge D|_U whose pullback coefficient is less than coeff⁡E(π∗D)+ε\operatorname{coeff}_E(\pi^*D)+\varepsilon. Choose a closed point x∈U∩π(E)x\in U\cap\pi(E). Shrink UU around xx so that mB=div⁡U(s)mB=\operatorname{div}_U(s) for some m>0m>0 and s∈K(X)∗s\in K(X)^*.

The positive part of mD−div⁡X(s)mD-\operatorname{div}_X(s) has finitely many prime components, none passing through xx. Quasi-projectivity supplies an effective Cartier divisor AA dominating this positive part and avoiding xx: take a sufficiently high ample twist of the ideal imposing these finitely many vanishing orders, and choose a section nonzero at xx. Then

C=div⁡X(s)+Am≥D.C=\frac{\operatorname{div}_X(s)+A}{m}\ge D.

Since xx lies in the center of EE and AA avoids xx, that center is not contained in AA, so coeff⁡E(π∗A)=0\operatorname{coeff}_E(\pi^*A)=0. It follows that

coeff⁡E(π∗C)=coeff⁡EU(πU∗B)<coeff⁡E(π∗D)+ε.\operatorname{coeff}_E(\pi^*C)=\operatorname{coeff}_{E_U}(\pi_U^*B)<\operatorname{coeff}_E(\pi^*D)+\varepsilon.

Monotonicity gives the reverse bound for every global upper bound. Letting ε→0\varepsilon\to0 proves the upper-envelope identity. Apply it to −D-D, and replace the upper bound of −D-D by the negative of a lower bound of DD, to obtain the supremum formula. The middle inequality is Property 7. The R\mathbb R-Cartier version follows by inclusion of families and monotonicity, as in Property 11.

Quasi-projectivity is used to construct AA. On a general complete normal variety the global upper-bound family can be empty; the local affine formula does not have this defect.

Property 13. Simultaneous approximation at finitely many valuations

Assume X,YX,Y are normal and quasi-projective. Given prime divisors E1,…,Er⊂YE_1,\ldots,E_r\subset Y and ε>0\varepsilon>0, there is one Q\mathbb Q-Cartier divisor C≥DC\ge D such that

0≤coeff⁡Ei(π∗C−π∗D)<ε(1≤i≤r).0\le\operatorname{coeff}_{E_i}(\pi^*C-\pi^*D)<\varepsilon\quad(1\le i\le r).

In particular, the same assertion holds allowing R\mathbb R-Cartier upper bounds.

Lemma 1.14, p. 5

Proof of Property 13

For each ii, Property 12 supplies a Q\mathbb Q-Cartier Ci≥DC_i\ge D with error less than ε\varepsilon along EiE_i. Choose m>0m>0 such that all mCimC_i are Cartier. Choose a sufficiently positive ample Cartier divisor AA so that every OX(A−mCi)\mathcal O_X(A-mC_i) is globally generated. Pick xix_i in the center of EiE_i and a section sis_i of this line bundle nonzero at xix_i. Regard sis_i as a rational function. Then

A+div⁡X(si)≥mCi≥mD,A+\operatorname{div}_X(s_i)\ge mC_i\ge mD,

and the effective divisor A+div⁡X(si)−mCiA+\operatorname{div}_X(s_i)-mC_i avoids xix_i. Its pullback has coefficient zero along EiE_i. Thus

coeff⁡Ei ⁣(π∗(A+div⁡X(si)))=mcoeff⁡Ei(π∗Ci).\operatorname{coeff}_{E_i}\!\left(\pi^*(A+\operatorname{div}_X(s_i))\right)=m\operatorname{coeff}_{E_i}(\pi^*C_i).

Take a general linear combination s=∑jλjsjs=\sum_j\lambda_js_j. At every prime divisor on XX, valuation of a sum is at least the minimum of the valuations of its terms. Hence A+div⁡X(s)≥mDA+\operatorname{div}_X(s)\ge mD. At each of the finitely many EiE_i, cancellation of the terms of least valuation is a proper linear condition on the coefficients λj\lambda_j. Since the ground field is infinite, choose the coefficients outside their finite union. Then, for C=(A+div⁡X(s))/mC=(A+\operatorname{div}_X(s))/m,

coeff⁡Ei(π∗C)=1mmin⁡jcoeff⁡Ei ⁣(π∗(A+div⁡X(sj)))≤coeff⁡Ei(π∗Ci).\operatorname{coeff}_{E_i}(\pi^*C)=\frac1m\min_j\operatorname{coeff}_{E_i}\!\left(\pi^*(A+\operatorname{div}_X(s_j))\right)\le\operatorname{coeff}_{E_i}(\pi^*C_i).

The upper estimate follows from the choice of CiC_i, and the lower estimate follows from C≥DC\ge D and monotonicity. This is one common upper bound, not separate choices for the different valuations.

Changing models and finite morphisms

Property 14. The composition inequality

Let ρ:Z→Y\rho:Z\to Y be another projective birational morphism of normal varieties. Then

ρ∗(π∗D)≤(π∘ρ)∗D,\rho^*(\pi^*D)\le(\pi\circ\rho)^*D,

and the difference is effective and ρ\rho-exceptional. It is zero when DD is R\mathbb R-Cartier.

Lemma 3.3, p. 14

Proof of Property 14

Fix a prime divisor F⊂ZF\subset Z and work over an affine open set of XX meeting its center. For every Q\mathbb Q-Cartier upper bound C≥DC\ge D, monotonicity gives π∗D≤π∗C\pi^*D\le\pi^*C, and hence

ρ∗(π∗D)≤ρ∗(π∗C)=(π∘ρ)∗C.\rho^*(\pi^*D)\le\rho^*(\pi^*C)=(\pi\circ\rho)^*C.

The equality uses the ordinary Cartier composition law, because CC and its pullback are Q\mathbb Q-Cartier. Taking coefficients along FF and then the infimum over CC gives the desired inequality by Property 11 for π∘ρ\pi\circ\rho.

If FF is nonexceptional for ρ\rho, its generic point maps to the generic point of a prime divisor of YY. The corresponding valuations of the common function field coincide. The coefficientwise definition in Property 1 therefore gives equal coefficients for the two pullbacks along FF. Thus the effective difference is ρ\rho-exceptional. If DD is R\mathbb R-Cartier, all pullbacks are ordinary Cartier pullbacks and compose exactly.

□

Property 15. Pushforward from a higher model

With the notation of Property 14,

ρ∗(π∘ρ)∗D=π∗D.\rho_*(\pi\circ\rho)^*D=\pi^*D.

The coefficient assigned to a fixed divisorial valuation is therefore independent of the model carrying that valuation.

Direct consequence of Property 14

Proof of Property 15

By Property 14, T=(π∘ρ)∗D−ρ∗(π∗D)T=(\pi\circ\rho)^*D-\rho^*(\pi^*D) is ρ\rho-exceptional, so ρ∗T=0\rho_*T=0. Property 8, now applied to ρ\rho and the Weil R\mathbb R-divisor π∗D\pi^*D on YY, gives ρ∗ρ∗(π∗D)=π∗D\rho_*\rho^*(\pi^*D)=\pi^*D. Combining these identities proves the formula.

For two models carrying a given prime divisor over XX, take a common normal projective birational model dominating both. Along the strict transforms of that divisor, pushforward preserves its coefficient. The displayed formula then identifies the two coefficients. This is compatibility under pushforward from higher models; it does not assert equality of iterated asymptotic pullbacks.

□

Property 16. Comparison with finite pushforward

Consider a commutative diagram of normal varieties

Y′→f′X′πY↓↓πXY→fX,\begin{array}{ccc}Y'&\xrightarrow{f'}&X'\\ {\scriptstyle\pi_Y}\downarrow&&\downarrow{\scriptstyle\pi_X}\\Y&\xrightarrow{f}&X,\end{array}

where f,f′f,f' are finite surjective and πX,πY\pi_X,\pi_Y are projective birational. For a Weil R\mathbb R-divisor DD on YY,

πX∗(f∗D)≤f∗′(πY∗D).\pi_X^*(f_*D)\le f'_*(\pi_Y^*D).

Equality holds if DD is R\mathbb R-Cartier.

Lemma 2.7, pp. 11–12

Proof of Property 16

First suppose DD is Cartier. Near any point of XX, semilocal triviality over the finite fibre and shrinking the base allow us to write D=div⁡Y(s)D=\operatorname{div}_Y(s). The norm formula gives f∗D=div⁡X(NK(Y)/K(X)s)f_*D=\operatorname{div}_X(N_{K(Y)/K(X)}s), so f∗Df_*D is Cartier. The two rows have the same function-field extension, hence

f∗′(πY∗D)=div⁡X′(NK(Y)/K(X)s)=πX∗(f∗D).f'_*(\pi_Y^*D)=\operatorname{div}_{X'}(N_{K(Y)/K(X)}s)=\pi_X^*(f_*D).

For completeness, the norm formula is checked at a codimension-one discrete valuation ring: multiplication by ss has determinant equal to its field norm, and its determinant order is the sum of the orders upstairs weighted by the residue-field degrees. Rational and R\mathbb R-Cartier divisors follow by linearity of ordinary pullback and pushforward.

For general DD, the claim is local on XX, so assume XX affine; its finite inverse image YY is affine as well. For each Q\mathbb Q-Cartier C≥DC\ge D, effectivity of finite pushforward, monotonicity, and the Cartier case give

πX∗(f∗D)≤πX∗(f∗C)=f∗′(πY∗C).\pi_X^*(f_*D)\le\pi_X^*(f_*C)=f'_*(\pi_Y^*C).

Fix E⊂X′E\subset X' and let F1,…,Fr⊂Y′F_1,\ldots,F_r\subset Y' be the primes over it, with residue degrees bib_i. Property 13 supplies one C≥DC\ge D with all errors coeff⁡Fi(πY∗C−πY∗D)\operatorname{coeff}_{F_i}(\pi_Y^*C-\pi_Y^*D) in [0,ε)[0,\varepsilon). Therefore

0≤coeff⁡E ⁣(f∗′(πY∗C)−f∗′(πY∗D))<ε∑ibi.0\le\operatorname{coeff}_E\!\left(f'_*(\pi_Y^*C)-f'_*(\pi_Y^*D)\right)<\varepsilon\sum_i b_i.

Use this in the preceding inequality and let ε→0\varepsilon\to0. This proves the claim along every EE. The simultaneous approximation is essential because several FiF_i may lie above the same EE.

□

Surfaces, convergence, and cones

Property 17. Surfaces and Mumford numerical pullback

If XX is a normal surface, then

π∗D=πMum∗D.\pi^*D=\pi^*_{\mathrm{Mum}}D.

Consequently, the asymptotic pullback is linear on surfaces and π∗(−D)=−π∗D\pi^*(-D)=-\pi^*D. For rational DD, its exceptional coefficients are rational.

Lemma 1.7(5), p. 2; discussion on p. 6

Proof of Property 17

We use the standard surface identification of nef envelopes, stated precisely in Boucksom–de Fernex–Favre, Theorem 2.22(ii): on a smooth model of a normal surface, the trace of Env⁡X(B)\operatorname{Env}_X(B) is Mumford's numerical pullback of BB. Here Env⁡X(B)\operatorname{Env}_X(B) is the nef envelope defined from the graded sequence of divisorial sheaves OX(mB)\mathcal O_X(mB); Remark 2.4 of the same paper identifies our pullback as

π∗D=−Env⁡X(−D)Y.\pi^*D=-\operatorname{Env}_X(-D)_Y.

On a resolution, Mumford's pullback N(D)N(D) is the unique divisor with pushforward DD and N(D)⋅Ei=0N(D)\cdot E_i=0 for all exceptional curves. Writing N(D)=D~+∑aiEiN(D)=\widetilde D+\sum a_iE_i, its coefficients solve

∑j(Ei⋅Ej)aj=−D~⋅Ei.\sum_j(E_i\cdot E_j)a_j=-\widetilde D\cdot E_i.

The exceptional intersection matrix is negative definite and thus invertible. This proves uniqueness, linear dependence on DD, and rationality of the aia_i for rational DD. In particular, N(−D)=−N(D)N(-D)=-N(D). The cited surface identification now gives π∗D=−N(−D)=N(D)\pi^*D=-N(-D)=N(D) on a resolution.

If YY is only normal, choose a resolution μ:W→Y\mu:W\to Y. Property 15 gives π∗D=μ∗(π∘μ)∗D\pi^*D=\mu_*(\pi\circ\mu)^*D, while Mumford's pullback on YY is defined by pushing forward its value on such a resolution. The equality on WW therefore descends to YY. The nef-envelope identification is the substantive external surface theorem used in this proof; it is not an assumption that DD is Cartier.

□

Property 18. Numerical convergence for effective divisors

Let XX be normal projective of dimension n>0n>0. If Ds≥0D_s\ge0 and their numerical classes [Ds][D_s] as (n−1)(n-1)-cycles tend to zero, then on each fixed normal projective birational model π:Y→X\pi:Y\to X,

π∗Ds⟶0coefficientwise.\pi^*D_s\longrightarrow0\quad\text{coefficientwise}.

No fixed-support hypothesis is needed here.

Lemma 4.2(2), pp. 16–17

Proof of Property 18

We first establish the Cartier majorant used in the argument. Fix a very ample Cartier divisor HH. There is an integer b>0b>0, depending only on X,HX,H, such that for each prime divisor QQ of degree d=Q⋅Hn−1d=Q\cdot H^{n-1} there is BQ∈∣bdH∣B_Q\in|bdH| containing QQ. Indeed, for an integral projective variety ZZ of dimension rr and degree dd, hyperplane restriction and induction give h0(Z,mH)≤d(m+rr)h^0(Z,mH)\le d\binom{m+r}{r}. For r=0r=0 this is the length bound; for positive rr, sum the restriction inequalities over mm, using a general integral hyperplane section when r≥2r\ge2 and a section of length dd when r=1r=1.

The Hilbert polynomial of XX allows bb so large that

h0(X,mH)>mb(m+n−1n−1)(m≥b).h^0(X,mH)>\frac mb\binom{m+n-1}{n-1}\quad(m\ge b).

Set m=bdm=bd. The right side is at least h0(Q,mH)h^0(Q,mH), so restriction to QQ has a nonzero kernel. The divisor of a nonzero section in that kernel is the required BQB_Q. Writing an effective real divisor as D=∑QdQQD=\sum_Q d_QQ and setting B=∑QdQBQB=\sum_Q d_QB_Q, we obtain

0≤D≤B,B∼Rb(D⋅Hn−1)H.0\le D\le B,\qquad B\sim_{\mathbb R}b(D\cdot H^{n-1})H.

Apply this to each DsD_s, obtaining BsB_s. Properties 3 and 6 give 0≤π∗Ds≤π∗Bs0\le\pi^*D_s\le\pi^*B_s. Fix an ample Cartier divisor AA on YY and a prime divisor F⊂YF\subset Y. Each prime divisor has positive intersection with An−1A^{n-1}, so

0≤coeff⁡F(π∗Ds)(F⋅An−1)≤π∗Bs⋅An−1=b(Ds⋅Hn−1)(π∗H⋅An−1).0\le\operatorname{coeff}_F(\pi^*D_s)(F\cdot A^{n-1})\le\pi^*B_s\cdot A^{n-1}=b(D_s\cdot H^{n-1})(\pi^*H\cdot A^{n-1}).

Divide by the positive constant F⋅An−1F\cdot A^{n-1}. Numerical convergence implies Ds⋅Hn−1→0D_s\cdot H^{n-1}\to0, proving the claim. Here effectivity and the majorants control moving supports; this is distinct from Property 10.

□

Property 19. The pseudo-effective threshold on a cone

Let XX be the projective cone over a smooth projective variety SS, formed using a sufficiently ample Cartier divisor LL. Let DD be the cone over an effective Cartier divisor BB on SS. For the blowup π:Y→X\pi:Y\to X of the vertex, with exceptional divisor EE,

π∗D=D~+τE,τ=inf⁡{t≥0:tL−B is pseudo-effective}.\pi^*D=\widetilde D+\tau E,\qquad\tau=\inf\{t\ge0:tL-B\text{ is pseudo-effective}\}.

If SS is an abelian variety of dimension g≥1g\ge1, then (τL−B)g=0(\tau L-B)^g=0, so τ\tau is algebraic over the rationals of degree at most gg.

Lemma 1.17, p. 7

Proof of Property 19

On the affine chart around the vertex, the cone is Spec⁡⨁k≥0H0(S,OS(kL))\operatorname{Spec}\bigoplus_{k\ge0}H^0(S,\mathcal O_S(kL)), and its blowup is the total space of OS(−L)\mathcal O_S(-L). The exceptional divisor is the zero section. The degree-kk part of the divisorial ideal OX(−mD)\mathcal O_X(-mD) is H0(S,OS(kL−mB))H^0(S,\mathcal O_S(kL-mB)). A nonzero homogeneous element of degree kk has order kk along EE. Consequently,

coeff⁡E(π∗D)=inf⁡{km:m≥1, k≥0, H0(S,OS(kL−mB))≠0}.\operatorname{coeff}_E(\pi^*D)=\inf\left\{\frac km:m\ge1,\ k\ge0,\ H^0(S,\mathcal O_S(kL-mB))\ne0\right\}.

Every ratio in this set is at least τ\tau, since a divisor with a nonzero section is pseudo-effective. Conversely, let t=k/m>τt=k/m>\tau be rational. By closedness of the pseudo-effective cone, τL−B\tau L-B is pseudo-effective; adding the ample real divisor (t−τ)L(t-\tau)L makes tL−BtL-B big. Thus the integral divisor kL−mBkL-mB is big, and some positive multiple has a nonzero section. Its ratio remains k/m=tk/m=t. Rational t↓τt\downarrow\tau now prove equality. Away from EE, Property 8 fixes the strict-transform coefficients.

If SS is abelian, its pseudo-effective cone equals its nef cone, so τL−B\tau L-B is nef. If τ>0\tau>0 and it were big, openness of the big cone would make (τ−δ)L−B(\tau-\delta)L-B big for small δ>0\delta>0, contradicting minimality of τ\tau. If τ=0\tau=0, the divisor −B-B is not big: its intersection with Lg−1L^{g-1} is nonpositive, whereas a big divisor has positive such degree. A nef divisor is big exactly when its top self-intersection is positive, hence (τL−B)g=0(\tau L-B)^g=0. The polynomial (tL−B)g(tL-B)^g has integral coefficients and leading coefficient Lg>0L^g>0, which proves the degree bound.

□

Examples and limitations

A. Natural versus asymptotic pullback (Example 1.8, p. 3). On X=(xy=z2)⊂A3X=(xy=z^2)\subset\mathbb A^3, put D=(x=z=0)D=(x=z=0) and blow up the vertex. Since 2D=div⁡X(x)2D=\operatorname{div}_X(x), the local equations on the yy-chart give

π♮D=D~+E,π∗D=D~+12E.\pi^\natural D=\widetilde D+E,\qquad\pi^*D=\widetilde D+\tfrac12E.

In fact coeff⁡E(π♮(mD)/m)=⌈m/2⌉/m\operatorname{coeff}_E(\pi^\natural(mD)/m)=\lceil m/2\rceil/m. The values 1,1/2,2/3,1/2,…1,1/2,2/3,1/2,\ldots show why convergence does not mean monotonicity.

B. Strict subadditivity and failure of oddness (Example 1.18, p. 7). On X=(xy=zw)⊂A4X=(xy=zw)\subset\mathbb A^4, let D=(x=z=0)D=(x=z=0) and D′=(x=w=0)D'=(x=w=0). The monomial calculation gives OX(−mD)=(x,z)m\mathcal O_X(-mD)=(x,z)^m, OX(−mD′)=(x,w)m\mathcal O_X(-mD')=(x,w)^m, and OX(mD)=x−m(x,w)m\mathcal O_X(mD)=x^{-m}(x,w)^m. Every coordinate has order one along the exceptional divisor of the vertex blowup. Hence

coeff⁡E(π∗D)=coeff⁡E(π∗D′)=1,coeff⁡E(π∗(−D))=0.\operatorname{coeff}_E(\pi^*D)=\operatorname{coeff}_E(\pi^*D')=1,\qquad\operatorname{coeff}_E(\pi^*(-D))=0.

Since D+D′=div⁡X(x)D+D'=\operatorname{div}_X(x), its pullback has coefficient one, whereas the sum of the two pullbacks has coefficient two. Moreover, natural homogeneity π♮(mD)=mπ♮D\pi^\natural(mD)=m\pi^\natural D holds on every normal birational model here, although DD is not Q\mathbb Q-Cartier.

C. Moving supports (p. 6). For π:Bl⁡0A2→A2\pi:\operatorname{Bl}_0\mathbb A^2\to\mathbb A^2, let LjL_j be pairwise distinct lines through the origin. Then Lj→0L_j\to0 coefficientwise, but coeff⁡E(π∗Lj)=1\operatorname{coeff}_E(\pi^*L_j)=1 for every jj. This does not contradict Property 18: the hypotheses there include projectivity and numerical convergence to zero.

D. An irrational coefficient and no minimizing upper bound (Example 1.16, pp. 6–7). Take S=A×AS=A\times A for an elliptic curve AA with origin 00, write F1=A×{0}F_1=A\times\{0\}, F2={0}×AF_2=\{0\}\times A, and let Δ\Delta be the diagonal. Set H=F1+F2H=F_1+F_2, B=F1+ΔB=F_1+\Delta, and L=NHL=NH for N≫0N\gg0. Here H2=B2=2H^2=B^2=2 and H⋅B=3H\cdot B=3, so the pseudo-effective boundary is detected by (tH−B)2=2(t2−3t+1)(tH-B)^2=2(t^2-3t+1) in the positive component. Property 19 gives

π∗D=D~+3+52NE.\pi^*D=\widetilde D+\frac{3+\sqrt5}{2N}E.

Each Q\mathbb Q-Cartier upper bound has rational pullback coefficient, so no finite family computes this infimum. Nor can a R\mathbb R-Cartier upper bound attain it: write such a bound as a finite sum ∑cjAj\sum c_jA_j. Feasibility is a rational polyhedron in the cjc_j, and the coefficient along EE is a rational linear functional. At an attained minimum it is constant on the affine space of active constraints, so a nearby rational point would attain the same irrational value, a contradiction.
